What the Adaptive Defensive Layer adds

The multi-round promo-fraud framework (TPNE-XGB) already models an adaptive attacker (intelligent generator) against an adaptive detector. This platform adds the missing third actor: an adaptive defense. Real institutions never just predict a fraud probability — they decide what to do about it. The ADL is the operational decision engine that converts P(fraud) into a concrete action:

Allowrisk < T₁
ReviewT₁ ≤ risk < T₂
Blockrisk ≥ T₂

The risk score combines five fraud indicators:

  • Pₛ — fraud probability predicted by the detector
  • C — graph centrality (fraudsters sit in important graph positions)
  • S — fraud-ring participation (connections to suspicious nodes)
  • V — transaction velocity (volume over a short window)
  • A — account trust score (older accounts = lower risk)

The thresholds T₁/T₂ are not fixed: after every round they move based on the trade-off between fraud escape and false blocking (T′ = T + α · (false_block − escape)). If too much fraud slips through the defense tightens; if too many genuine users are blocked it loosens.

The feedback loop

The critical design decision: blocked fraud never reaches the attacker. It is soft-removed from the graph, excluded from every feature, metric and training split, and never appears in the generator’s memory. Only fraud that survives the whole defense becomes the next round’s training material — so the intelligent generator must keep evolving against an active defense that is itself learning.

Pipeline

build world
train detector
predict Pₛ
risk score R
allow / review / block
remove blocked
generator learns from survivors
synthesise new fraud
adapt thresholds
retrain & repeat

Honesty notes

  • Ground truth is budgeted: only a few new accounts are “manually reviewed” each round; the rest stay unseen (limited labelling).
  • The Review decision is probabilistic: a configurable fraction (review_catch_rate) of reviewed fraud is caught, the rest is allowed after investigation.
  • Decision latency is simulated: per-transaction cost differs by action (review is the most expensive), exposing the operational cost of a cautious defense.
  • Run the same config with fixed thresholds or ADL disabled as baselines — the History tab compares F1 / AUC / escape / false-block side by side.
  • All data is synthetic; this is an independent implementation of the ADL concept.
